## 1. Executive Summary

Website Operations is the business model — the Founder Package's "$500 is how
we earn the monthly relationship" points *here* — yet it's the least defined
thing on the site. Today it's described in enterprise language ("managed
website operations for revenue-critical sites," "Capacity Units," "audit
trails") to an audience of roofers, landscapers, salons, and restaurants. The
infrastructure is real and strong (one request desk, human approval, change
records, the operator console). The product wrapper is missing.

The fix mirrors the Missed Jobs Report rename: sell it in the customer's own
format. Every trade and local service already sells *exactly this product* —
the maintenance plan, the service agreement, the priority club.
**Website Operations is a maintenance plan for your website.** The customer
thought it answers is not "my website is broken" (it isn't) but **"I never
update it"** and **"I don't have time to deal with it."**

This document defines what the customer gets each month in concrete terms, a
three-tier structure mapped onto existing prices, the journey from lead to
long-term retention (with the make-or-break "first win" moment designed), the
hand-offs from the Founder Package (day 21) and the Missed Jobs Report (the
roadmap), and a 25-question FAQ. One new customer-facing concept does heavy
lifting throughout: the **monthly "What We Did" summary** — the recurring
proof that makes a maintenance subscription feel obviously worth keeping.

## 2. Website Operations — Definition

### What is it?

A monthly service where Website Support Studio takes care of a business's
website: updates, edits, fixes, small improvements, and watchfulness —
requested through one desk, done by an operator, approved by a human before
going live, and reported back in plain English. The customer's job shrinks to
two actions: *ask for what they want* and *approve what they see.*

In owner language: **"Your website, handled."** Or one notch more concrete:
*"Text us the change. We make it. You see it before it goes live."*

### What problem does it solve?

Websites don't fail loudly — they go stale. The phone number changes on
Google but not the site. The hours are wrong for a holiday. The "before"
photos from a great job never go up. A form quietly breaks in March and
nobody notices until June. The owner knows the site needs touching and has
known for months — but there's no one whose job it is, the old web guy
vanished, and every small change costs a research project ("how do I even
log into this thing?").

The problem is not a bad website. The problem is **an unowned website.**
Operations gives it an owner whose job never lapses.

### Who is it for?

- Local service businesses with a working website — the trades plus the
  expanding model-gallery verticals (landscaping, salons, restaurants).
- Founder Website customers at launch + 30 days (the designed default path).
- Owners whose site is "fine" but perpetually three months out of date.
- Businesses whose Missed Jobs Report roadmap is full of website-tagged
  fixes they'll never do themselves.
- Anyone who has said "I have a guy" and then "...but I can't reach him."

### Who is it NOT for?

- **Businesses with no website** → Founder Website Package first.
- **Businesses that don't know what's wrong** and want answers, not hands →
  Missed Jobs Report first (§8).
- **Full redesigns, custom apps, e-commerce builds** → scoped separately;
  Operations handles ongoing care, not construction projects.
- **Owners who genuinely enjoy DIY-ing their site weekly** — they don't need
  us, and saying so plainly is cheap trust.
- **Hands-off-everything marketing buyers** who actually want ads management
  and lead generation — different product category; Operations runs the
  website, not the ad budget.

## 3. Positioning

### The thought we position against

Not *"my website is fine"* — that thought never buys anything. The buying
thoughts are the two that follow it:

> "My website is fine… **I just never update it.**"
> "My website is fine… **I just don't have time to deal with it.**"

Positioning sentence (the page-top line):

> **You sell maintenance plans. This is one — for your website.**
> Updates, fixes, and small improvements, handled every month by people whose
> job it is. You ask; we do it; nothing goes live without your eyes on it.

Why this frame wins: an HVAC owner doesn't have to be convinced that
maintenance contracts are worth paying for — they *sell* that argument every
week ("small problems get caught before they're big ones; you stop thinking
about it"). The format is pre-sold; we just point it at the website. (Same
move as the Missed Jobs Report's "service call for your lead flow" — their
own business model, handed back.)

Supporting positioning lines:
- "When did your website last get touched? Your customers can tell."
- "Every business has a website. Almost nobody has someone whose *job* is
  the website."
- "Stop being your own web guy."
- Against the disappeared freelancer: "One desk. A record of every change.
  Nobody to chase."

Vocabulary rules (consistent with the established owner-language standard):
"maintenance plan for your website," "requests," "changes," "your monthly
allowance," "what we did this month." The words "operations," "Capacity
Units," "audit trail," "deterministic lifecycle," and "revenue-critical"
stay internal — they're how we *run* it, not how we *sell* it.

## 4. Deliverables — what a customer actually receives each month

### The work (request-driven)

One desk for anything on this menu — submitted by email or from their
account, acknowledged next business day (existing published standard):

- **Updates:** hours, phone, pricing, staff, service lists, seasonal banners
  ("Book your fall tune-up"), holiday notices
- **Content edits:** new photos from real jobs, project galleries, page copy
  changes, new testimonials/reviews added to the site
- **Fixes:** broken links, broken forms, layout glitches, mobile issues,
  "the map is wrong," "the button does nothing"
- **Small improvements:** call-to-action tweaks, a new service-area page,
  landing-page adjustments, click-to-call placement, speed cleanups
- **Tracking care:** keeping analytics, form, and call tracking actually
  reporting (the Missed Jobs Report's most common findings, maintained)

### The watchfulness (standing, not request-driven) `PROPOSED`

What makes it a *plan* rather than a punch-card:
- **Monthly form test** — a real submission through every form, verified to
  arrive ("your form works" is checked, not assumed — the #1 silent failure)
- **Uptime + SSL monitoring** with our response, not just an alert
- **Monthly look-over** — links, mobile rendering, anything visibly stale

### The proof (the retention engine) `PROPOSED`

- **The monthly "What We Did" summary** — a plain-English email: changes
  made, checks run ("form tested on the 12th — arrived in 41 seconds"),
  anything we recommend for next month. One page, no dashboard required.
  This is the deliverable that re-sells the subscription twelve times a year;
  a maintenance plan that never shows its work always feels cancellable.

### The guardrails (existing, restated in owner language)

- Nothing goes live without human approval, and every change is recorded —
  who asked, who approved, what shipped, when.
- You own everything, always; cancelling stops the service, not your website
  (existing ownership terms apply unchanged).

## 8. Relationship to the Missed Jobs Report

The two products answer different sentences:

> **"Something's wrong and I don't know what."** → Missed Jobs Report
> (diagnosis: evidence, score, prioritized roadmap — $750 one-time)
>
> **"I know what needs doing — I just need it done, and kept done."** →
> Website Operations (execution: the standing crew)

Routing rules (publish as one simple FAQ answer, run as sales discipline):

1. Prospect lists *specific known tasks* ("hours are wrong, need new photos,
   form might be broken") → **Operations.** Selling them a $750 diagnostic
   first would be padding; the form check happens in week one anyway.
2. Prospect describes *symptoms* ("phone's quiet," "ads aren't working,"
   "no idea if the website does anything") → **Missed Jobs Report.** Selling
   them Operations first risks competently maintaining a leaking system.
3. **Report → Operations** is the designed flow: website-tagged roadmap
   items become the first three months of Operations requests — the
   subscription arrives with its to-do list pre-written and dollar-justified.
4. **Operations → Report** happens too: a maintained site whose owner starts
   asking "but is it *working*?" gets the diagnostic — and an Operations
   customer buying the Report gets their roadmap executed without re-buying
   capacity they already pay for.

The Fix-It Sprint ($1,500–$3,500) sits between for one-time *project-sized*
repairs; Operations is for the never-ending stream of small ones. Sprint
builds it; Operations keeps it built.

## 9. Top 25 FAQs (copy final; `PROPOSED` items inherit §4–§6 pending approval)

1. **What is Website Operations?**
   A monthly maintenance plan for your website. You send requests — updates,
   edits, fixes, improvements — and we do them, with every change reviewed
   by a human before it goes live and reported back to you in plain English.

2. **What counts as a request?**
   A single, scoped piece of work: change the hours, add photos from last
   week's job, fix the form, add a page for a town you serve, put up the
   seasonal banner, adjust a headline. If you'd say it in one sentence, it's
   probably a request.

3. **What's NOT included?**
   Construction projects: full redesigns, new custom features, online
   booking systems, e-commerce, custom apps. We'll scope those separately
   before any work starts — and tell you plainly when something you asked
   for crosses that line.

4. **How much does it cost?**
   $399/month for one website. Founder Website customers pay the founder
   rate — $199/month for months 2–7, then $399. Up to five websites is
   $899/month. More than that, we scope custom.

5. **How much work is included each month?**
   A monthly allowance that comfortably covers what most businesses send —
   roughly 8–15 typical requests. Small changes use a little of it, bigger
   jobs use more, and your monthly summary always shows where you stand.

6. **What if I have a busy month and run out?**
   Add a top-up anytime — extra work credit that rolls over until used.
   Your regular monthly allowance refreshes every month.

7. **What if I barely use it some months?**
   The standing care still runs — your forms get tested, your site gets
   monitored and looked over, and you get the summary. Quiet months are
   what maintenance plans are for; that's true of the ones you sell, too.

8. **How fast do you respond?**
   Requests are acknowledged the next business day and prioritized by
   urgency. Genuinely urgent problems take an expedited path.

9. **What if my website goes down or something breaks badly?**
   That's the urgent path — report it and it jumps the line. With monitoring
   on your site, we often know before you do.

10. **Do you check my forms?**
    Yes — every month we submit a real test through your forms and confirm
    it arrives. A silently broken form is the most expensive website problem
    there is, and the one nobody notices.

11. **How do I send a request?**
    Email us or use your account — whichever you'll actually use. It lands
    on one desk either way, gets an owner, and you can see its status.

12. **Will you change things without asking me?**
    No. Nothing goes live without human approval, and every change is
    recorded — what changed, who asked, who approved, when. Routine checks
    (like form tests) run automatically, but changes to your site don't.

13. **How do I know what I'm paying for each month?**
    The monthly "What We Did" summary: every change made, every check run,
    and anything we recommend — one page, plain English.

14. **Can I cancel?**
    Anytime. Cancelling stops the service, not your website — your site,
    domain, and accounts are yours and stay yours. It's in our Terms, in
    writing.

15. **Do you hold my website hostage if I leave?**
    No — the opposite, on purpose. Take it anywhere, export everything, or
    keep it hosted under a simple arrangement. We'll even help you migrate
    if you ask. We'd rather be easy to leave than scary to join.

16. **My website wasn't built by you. Can you still run it?**
    Usually, yes — WordPress, Webflow, and most standard setups are core
    territory. We'll confirm fit before you pay, not after.

17. **Who actually does the work?**
    Website Support Studio operators — run by Gary Corriston, Corriston
    Consulting, LLC, Tampa, Florida. Not a marketplace, not a ticket farm.
    A person answers, and the same desk sees every request.

18. **Do you write the words and edit the images?**
    Yes — request-sized writing and image work is included: a new section's
    copy, a reworded headline, photos cropped/optimized and placed. Full
    site copywriting from scratch is a scoped project.

19. **Do you do SEO?**
    The maintenance layer of it, yes: titles, descriptions, schema, new
    service-area pages, technical fixes, keeping Search Console healthy.
    Full SEO campaigns are a separate engagement — and we'll say which is
    which before any work starts.

20. **Do you manage my Google Ads or social media?**
    No. Operations runs your website. Tracking on your site (so you can see
    what your ads produce) — yes, we keep that working. Running ad budgets —
    different product, and we'd rather be excellent at this one.

21. **How is this different from my nephew / a freelancer / an agency?**
    Availability and accountability. A freelancer is great until they're
    busy; an agency is great until you're small. This is one desk where
    requests can't fall through cracks, every change is recorded, a human
    approves everything, and the price doesn't surprise you.

22. **How is this different from the Missed Jobs Report?**
    The Report is a diagnosis — where you're losing jobs, with evidence, in
    a one-time $750 report. Operations is the crew — the ongoing doing.
    Don't know what's wrong? Report. Know it just needs handling? Operations.
    Many customers do the Report once, then Operations executes its fix list.

23. **I just launched a Founder Website. Why would I need this?**
    Your first 30 days of fixes and tweaks are already included — Operations
    is that, continuing: seasonal updates, new job photos, new pages as you
    grow, forms tested monthly, someone whose job your website is. Your
    founder rate ($199/month for months 2–7) is the lowest it will ever be.

24. **What happens at month 8?**
    The founder rate steps up to the standard $399/month. You'll see
    reminders well before it happens — no surprise on the invoice — and you
    can cancel anytime before or after.

25. **Can I see what a month actually looks like before I buy?**
    Yes — we'll show you a sample "What We Did" summary so you can see the
    real shape of a month: the changes, the checks, the recommendations.
    If that page doesn't look worth the price to you, don't buy it.
