**TAMPA, Fla. — June 8, 2026** — Corriston Consulting, LLC today introduced **Website Support Studio**, a managed website operations and support platform for organizations whose websites are tied directly to revenue. Website Support Studio consolidates the requests, approvals, and execution work that normally scatter across inboxes, agencies, and internal queues into a single operational desk — with documented response workflows and a named owner for every change.

The platform is built for marketing leadership, marketing operations, digital teams, and IT stakeholders at organizations that cannot afford for website work to stall, get lost, or ship without review. It is not a freelancer marketplace and not a do-it-yourself ticketing tool. It is an operating model for keeping a production website reliable, accountable, and moving forward.

> A revenue-critical website is infrastructure. It deserves the same operational discipline as any other system the business depends on.

## What Website Support Studio is

Website Support Studio is a managed service layered on a purpose-built operations platform. Organizations submit website, marketing, and operations requests to one desk. Each request becomes a tracked unit of work that moves through a defined lifecycle — intake, triage, drafted response or change, human approval, execution, and closure — with an audit trail at every step.

The platform organizes work along a clear hierarchy of **client, site, and request**, so that ownership is never ambiguous. Every request has a status, an owner, and a record of who approved what and when. Nothing reaches a live website without passing through an explicit approval gate.

## The problem: websites generate revenue, but their support does not match their importance

For most organizations, the website is one of the highest-leverage assets the business owns. It generates pipeline, processes transactions, supports customers, and represents the brand. Yet the way website work actually gets done rarely reflects that importance.

The common failure pattern looks like this:

- **Websites generate revenue, but support is treated as overhead.** A page that converts customers is maintained with the same urgency as an internal wiki edit.
- **Support is fragmented.** Requests live in email threads, chat messages, project tools, and verbal hallway asks. No one can see the full queue, and work falls through the gaps.
- **Agencies disappear.** External partners rotate staff, deprioritize smaller accounts, or wind down — and the institutional knowledge of how the site is wired leaves with them.
- **Internal teams are overloaded.** The people who could make a change are already responsible for campaigns, releases, and incidents. Website maintenance competes for attention it rarely wins.
- **Ownership is unclear.** When something breaks, the first hour is spent deciding whose job it is to fix it. Accountability is diffuse precisely when it matters most.

The result is predictable: small problems linger, changes ship without review, and the website slowly drifts away from what the business needs it to be. The cost is real but hard to see, because it shows up as missed conversions and degraded trust rather than a single line item.

## The Website Support Studio approach

Website Support Studio replaces the fragmented pattern with a single operating model built on four commitments: operational visibility, managed support, defined response workflows, and human accountability.

### Operational visibility

Every request enters one desk and becomes visible work. Stakeholders can see what has been submitted, what stage each request is in, who owns it, and what is waiting on a decision. The queue is no longer a collection of private inboxes; it is a shared operational view. Visibility is the precondition for everything else — work that cannot be seen cannot be prioritized, measured, or trusted.

### Managed support

Website Support Studio is a managed service, not self-service software handed over and left alone. An operator-led support process, managed by Corriston Consulting, triages incoming requests, prepares the response or change, and carries the work through to completion. The organization gets the outcome — a fixed link, an updated page, a corrected form, a verified tracking change — without having to staff, train, and supervise the people doing it.

### Response workflows

Each request follows a deterministic lifecycle with clear entry and exit criteria at every stage: received, triaged, response drafted, awaiting approval, approved, delivered, and closed. The workflow is the same whether the request is a one-line copy change or a multi-step operational fix, which makes the process predictable and auditable. Exceptions — missing information, unclear scope, urgent production issues, sensitive data — have defined handling paths rather than ad hoc improvisation.

### Accountability

No customer-facing change is published without a mandatory human approval. Approval is a gate, not a formality: the platform records who reviewed the work, what they approved, and when. Every request carries its own evidence trail — source, classification, drafted response, approval decision, delivery confirmation, and closure note. When someone asks "who changed this, and who signed off," the answer is already recorded.

> The difference between a support queue and an operating model is accountability. Website Support Studio is built around the approval gate, not bolted on after it.

## Why website operations matters

Treating website work as operations rather than as a series of favors changes the economics. When requests are visible, owned, and tracked, prioritization becomes possible: the work that protects revenue gets done first, and the rest is scheduled rather than forgotten. When changes pass through review, defects and brand inconsistencies are caught before customers see them. When every action is recorded, the organization builds an institutional memory of its own website that does not walk out the door when a vendor or employee leaves.

For regulated and enterprise environments, the audit trail is not a nicety. Being able to demonstrate who approved a change to a revenue-critical surface — and when — is increasingly an expectation from security, compliance, and leadership. Website Support Studio makes that evidence a byproduct of doing the work, rather than a separate burden.

## How Website Support Studio originated

Website Support Studio did not start as a product idea. It started as an operational reality at Corriston Consulting, LLC, where the same patterns appeared across client after client: capable teams, important websites, and no shared system for getting website work done reliably. Requests arrived through every channel imaginable. Approvals happened in side conversations. Knowledge about how each site was built lived in a handful of heads.

The firm built an internal operating model to solve its own problem — a single intake, a defined workflow, a mandatory approval before anything shipped, and a complete record of every decision. That model worked, and it was the discipline, not any single tool, that made the difference. Website Support Studio is that operating model, built into a platform and offered as a managed service so that other organizations can adopt it without having to invent it.

## Future vision

Website Support Studio launches as a managed operations desk, and its roadmap extends along the same principle that started it: more reliability through more visibility and accountability, never less control. Planned directions include deeper operational reporting so leaders can see throughput and response performance over time, expanded request types as more of the website operations surface is brought into the desk, and assisted drafting that accelerates the support process while keeping the human approval gate firmly in place. Autonomy, where it is introduced at all, will be opt-in and bounded — the approval gate is the product, not an obstacle to be removed.

The long-term goal is straightforward: make it normal for a revenue-critical website to be operated with the same discipline as any other critical system the business runs.
