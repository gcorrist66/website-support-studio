## 0. Purpose & guiding principle

This framework exists to answer one question for every candidate: **"Is this a business we should spend effort to win?"**

The goal is **not** to find the most businesses. The goal is to find the businesses most likely to (a) need a better website, (b) value professional support, (c) have budget, (d) respond to outreach, and (e) convert into recurring revenue.

**Quality over quantity is the rule, not a preference.** Volume for its own sake is explicitly discouraged (see §11).

### How WSS sells (context for scoring)
1. Prospect finds WSS → 2. Prospect receives outreach → 3. Prospect requests a **Free Website Preview** → 4. WSS builds the preview → 5. Prospect becomes a redesign / support / management client (recurring revenue).

Because the conversion event is a **Free Website Preview request**, the best prospect is one whose **current website visibly underperforms** *and* whose **business is valuable enough to pay for ongoing help**. The scoring model below is built around exactly that pairing.

## 1. Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

**Primary ICP types**
- Local service businesses
- Professional service firms
- Multi-location businesses
- Small businesses
- Growing businesses

**Firmographics**
- **Employees:** 3–100
- **Annual revenue:** $500K–$25M

**Required business characteristics** (all should be true for a full-fit prospect)
- A website **exists** (WSS improves; it does not cold-build for businesses with no site — see §3).
- The business is **active** (recent signals of operation — see "business-alive validation," §10).
- The business **depends on leads / customers found online**.
- The **website influences revenue** (it's a real channel, not a vanity page).
- A **decision maker is identifiable** (owner/principal/office manager reachable).

A candidate that fails any *required characteristic* is not full-ICP and should be down-tiered or disqualified per §3.

## 2. Priority Industries

Industry fit is a scored input (§4, 0–10) and an Apollo targeting input (§8). Two tiers:

**Tier A (highest fit — lead-dependent, website influences revenue, clear budget):**
Roofing · HVAC · Electrical · Plumbing · Landscaping · Tree Service · Pool Contractors · Remodeling · Construction · Pressure Washing · Pest Control · Flooring · Painting · Auto Repair · Dentists · Chiropractors · Physical Therapy · Veterinary · Med Spa · Law Firms · Real Estate · Property Management

**Tier B (good fit — pursue, slightly lower priority):**
Restaurants · Salons · Fitness · Child Care · Senior Care · Accounting · Insurance · Mortgage · Financial Advisors · B2B Services · Manufacturing

Industries outside both tiers are not targeted by default; if encountered, treat industry fit as low (§4) and require strong website-opportunity + business-value signals to pursue.

---

## 3. Disqualification Rules (hard stops)

Automatically **disqualify** (do not score, do not pursue) when any is true:
- Out of business / no signs of activity
- No website at all
- Government agency
- Public school / school district
- University / college
- Nonprofit / NGO
- Fortune 500 company
- Enterprise organization (broadly: >100 employees or clearly enterprise-tier)
- Business with a **dedicated in-house web/dev team** (they don't need WSS)

When disqualifying, the agent **must record the disqualification reason** (§11).

## 4. Scoring Model — Prospect Opportunity & Fit Score (0–100)

> **Read the direction carefully — this is an OPPORTUNITY score, not a website report card.**
> A **higher score = a stronger prospect.** Points are awarded for the *size of the improvement opportunity* (a weaker current website earns MORE points, because there is more for WSS to fix and a stronger reason the owner says yes to a free preview) plus *industry fit*. This is why a weak website lands in the top tier (§5) — it is consistent with "poor website + strong business = top priority" (§9).
> Business *value* (reviews, locations, growth, marketing spend) is scored separately as the **Business Value qualifier** (§7) and is used to prioritize within a tier and confirm budget.

| Category | Max | What it measures | Scoring direction |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Website Design Quality | 20 | Visual modernity, polish, brand credibility | More points = weaker/dated design |
| Mobile Experience | 20 | Responsiveness, speed, usability on phone | More points = worse mobile |
| Conversion Quality | 20 | Clear CTA, quote/booking path, lead capture | More points = weaker conversion path |
| Trust Signals | 15 | Reviews, credentials, proof, real photos | More points = fewer trust signals |
| Local SEO Signals | 15 | NAP, Google Business presence, local relevance | More points = weaker local presence |
| Industry Fit | 10 | Tier A/B match (§2) | More points = better fit |
| **Total** | **100** | **Opportunity & Fit** | **Higher = stronger prospect** |

### 4.1 Sub-criteria (observable, justified — agents must base scores on what they can actually see)

**Website Design Quality (0–20)** — *justification: dated design is the #1 reason owners feel their site "looks unprofessional," the core WSS pitch.*
- 0–4: modern, professionally designed, on-brand → little opportunity
- 5–10: dated but functional; template-looking
- 11–15: clearly outdated (old layout, clip-art, inconsistent styling)
- 16–20: very outdated / generic stock site / looks abandoned

**Mobile Experience (0–20)** — *justification: most local-service traffic is mobile; poor mobile = lost leads = strong, demonstrable pitch.*
- 0–4: fully responsive, fast, easy to use on phone
- 5–10: responsive but slow or awkward
- 11–15: not truly mobile-optimized (pinch/zoom, broken layout)
- 16–20: unusable or broken on mobile

**Conversion Quality (0–20)** — *justification: WSS sells leads/bookings; no clear path to act is the highest-value fix.*
- 0–4: clear CTA + working quote/booking/contact path
- 5–10: a CTA exists but is weak/buried
- 11–15: no booking or quote path; only a generic contact form
- 16–20: no CTA at all / broken forms / no way to convert

**Trust Signals (0–15)** — *justification: trust gaps directly suppress conversion for service businesses.*
- 0–3: strong reviews, credentials, real photos, guarantees
- 4–8: some trust elements, thin
- 9–12: few/none; stock imagery only
- 13–15: no reviews, no proof, no credibility markers

**Local SEO Signals (0–15)** — *justification: a business invisible locally is both an opportunity and a sign WSS can add measurable value.*
- 0–3: strong local presence (GBP, NAP consistent, local content)
- 4–8: partial local signals
- 9–12: weak/missing local optimization
- 13–15: no discernible local presence

**Industry Fit (0–10)** — *justification: fit predicts response + budget; this is the one category where more points = better, not weaker.*
- 10: Tier A industry (§2)
- 6: Tier B industry
- 2: in-ICP but outside both tiers
- 0: outside ICP (usually disqualified at §3)

> The §6 red-flag checklist is the practical instrument for assigning the five website categories above (each red flag present pushes that category's score up). The §7 green flags feed the separate Business Value qualifier.

## 5. Prospect Tiers

Tiers are assigned on the **Opportunity & Fit Score (§4)**. Tier definitions match the rule "weak website + high value = pursue first."

| Tier | Score | Definition | Profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | 80–100 | **Immediate outreach** | Weak website · high business value · strong fit · clear opportunity |
| **B** | 60–79 | **Good outreach target** | Real opportunity, solid fit; pursue in normal flow |
| **C** | 40–59 | **Future opportunity** | Decent site or weaker fit; revisit later / nurture |
| **D** | <40 | **Do not actively pursue** | Strong site or poor fit; effort better spent elsewhere |

**Business-value gate:** within a tier, order and qualify using the Business Value qualifier (§7) and the priority order (§9). A site that scores Tier A on website weakness but shows **no** business-value signals (no reviews, single location, no growth, no marketing spend) may lack budget — down-prioritize it below value-rich peers, and record the reasoning.

---

## 6. Website Red Flags (drive the Opportunity score UP)

These are **negative judgments of the current website.** Each one present means the site is underperforming — which **increases** the prospect's Opportunity & Fit Score (§4) because it is exactly what WSS fixes. Record which are observed.

- Outdated design
- Slow website
- Broken forms
- No CTA
- No booking system
- No quote request path
- No trust signals
- Poor mobile experience
- Thin content
- Missing reviews
- Generic / stock template website

*Many red flags = strong opportunity = higher tier. This is the primary engine of the score.*

---

## 7. Website Green Flags — Business Value qualifier (scored positively, separate axis)

These are **positive judgments of the business** (not the website). They do not change the §4 website score; they raise **Business Value**, which sets priority within a tier (§9), confirms budget, and protects against pursuing weak-site businesses that can't pay.

- Active business (recent activity)
- Good reviews (volume + rating)
- Strong service offering
- Multiple locations
- Lead-generation focus
- Growth indicators (hiring, new locations, expanding services)
- Marketing investment (running ads, agency presence, active social)

**Business Value bands (qualitative):** High (several green flags, clear budget) · Medium · Low (few/none → budget risk).

## 8. Apollo Filter Recommendations

> Recommendations only. Per the operating constraints, agents **do not change Apollo account settings** — these are the inputs to use when *building a search*.

- **Company size:** 3–100 employees → Apollo `organization_num_employees_ranges`: `["1,10","11,50"]` (and optionally `["51,100"]`)
- **Location:** United States → `person_locations`: `["United States"]`
- **Industries:** Tier A + Tier B (§2)
- **Titles:** Owner · Founder · President · Managing Partner · Partner · CEO · Office Manager · Marketing Director → `person_titles` accordingly; `person_seniorities`: `["owner","founder","partner","c_suite"]`
- **Exclude:** Enterprise · Government · Education · Nonprofit (down-tier/disqualify per §3)
- **Prefer:** `contact_email_status`: `["verified"]` to protect deliverability.

**Validated NAICS starting points** (confirmed to return large US owner pools on 2026-06-18; NAICS must be 2–5 digits):
- Home services / trades: `["238","236","5617"]`
- Professional services: `["5411","5412","5242","531"]` (legal, accounting, insurance, real estate)
- Personal / local / health: `["8121","7225","71394","6212"]` (personal care, restaurants, fitness, dentists)

*Note: Apollo search masks last names; reveal requires enrichment. Enrich on first-name + company (or LinkedIn URL when available). Validate hit-rate on a small batch before enriching at volume.*

## 9. Outreach Prioritization

Within and across tiers, pursue in this order (poor website is the constant; business-value signal is the differentiator):

1. Poor website **+ strong business**
2. Poor website **+ active reviews**
3. Poor website **+ multiple locations**
4. Poor website **+ visible marketing spend**
5. Poor website **+ growth signals**

This operationalizes §5's business-value gate: the weaker the site and the more valuable/active the business, the sooner it is contacted.

---

## 10. Free Website Preview Eligibility

A business qualifies for a Free Website Preview offer when **all** are true:
- A website **exists**
- The website **can be meaningfully improved** (≥ moderate Opportunity score; at least several §6 red flags)
- A **decision maker can be identified**
- The business **appears active** (business-alive validation: recent reviews, current hours, live site, recent posts/updates, or other proof of operation)
- The business **fits the WSS ICP** (§1) and is not disqualified (§3)

If any is false, do not extend the preview offer; record why.

---

## 11. Agent Operating Rules

All agents (Apollo, Cowork, Codex, Claude, Hermes, future) **must**:
- **Score every prospect before outreach** using §4.
- **Record qualification reasoning** (why this prospect, score breakdown).
- **Record website observations** (which §6 red flags / §7 green flags were seen).
- **Record disqualification reasons** when applicable (§3, §10).
- **Prioritize quality over quantity** (§9).
- **Avoid scraping low-value businesses simply to increase volume.** A long list of Tier-C/D records is a failure, not progress.

**Minimum record per prospect:** business name · industry (tier) · decision maker · website URL · Opportunity & Fit score (with category breakdown) · business-value band · red/green flags observed · tier · pursue/hold/disqualify decision + reason.

## Appendix A — Worked example (illustrative)

*"Sunrise Roofing," 12 employees, single location, dated 2014-era template site, no online booking, only a contact form, 47 Google reviews at 4.8★, runs seasonal Facebook ads.*
- Design 16 (very dated) · Mobile 13 (not optimized) · Conversion 15 (no quote/booking path) · Trust 6 (has reviews but not surfaced on site) · Local SEO 9 (GBP exists, on-site weak) · Industry Fit 10 (Tier A roofing) → **Opportunity & Fit = 69 → Tier B**, but **Business Value = High** (47 reviews, active ads, growing) → **priority order #1/#4** → **pursue immediately, eligible for Free Website Preview.**
