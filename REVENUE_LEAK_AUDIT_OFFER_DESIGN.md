## 2. Revenue Leak Audit — Definition

### What is it?

A fixed-price, fixed-scope diagnostic that traces a local service business's
revenue path — from *"someone in your service area needs this service"* to
*"booked, completed, paid job"* — and identifies exactly where money falls out
of that path. It audits eight categories (website conversion, forms, calls,
tracking, attribution, lead quality, follow-up, and local visibility), scores
each with evidence, and delivers a prioritized, dollar-estimated repair plan.

It is **not** a website redesign pitch, an SEO report export, or a teardown of
aesthetics. Every finding must connect to a lost lead, an unmeasured lead, or a
mishandled lead.

### What business problem does it solve?

The owner of a $500K–$5M local service business typically cannot answer:

- How many calls did we miss last month, and what were they worth?
- Does our contact form actually work — and how fast do we respond?
- Which marketing dollars produce booked jobs (not clicks — jobs)?
- Do we show up at all in the towns we drive to every day?
- What happens to a quote we sent and never heard back about?

Because nothing is measured, every marketing decision is a guess, and every
operational gap (missed call, dead form, 2-day response) silently repeats. The
audit converts "marketing feels like it isn't working" into a ranked list of
specific, fixable leaks with dollar estimates — making the *next* dollar the
business spends an informed one.

### Who is it for?

- Local service businesses: roofing, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and adjacent
  trades (matches the WSS template verticals and the 72-prospect list).
- Roughly $500K–$5M annual revenue; owner-operated or small office staff.
- Already generating demand: a functioning website, live phone line, some ad
  spend, SEO, or steady word-of-mouth lead flow.
- Spending on marketing **without attribution** — they buy ads/SEO/lead-gen but
  can't tie spend to booked jobs.
- Scored as "keep your website" in the Website Replacement Score audit — the
  site is serviceable; the system around it is not.

### Who is it NOT for?

- **Pre-revenue or no-traffic businesses.** Nothing to audit; they need the
  $500 Founder Website Package first (the 16 prospects).
- **Businesses that genuinely need a replacement site.** Auditing a condemned
  building is malpractice; route them to the Founder Website Package, then
  audit after launch if warranted.
- **E-commerce, SaaS, or national lead-gen operations.** The framework is built
  around local, call-and-form, quote-then-job economics.
- **Organizations with mature analytics/RevOps teams.** They already have the
  instrumentation; this is not an enterprise analytics engagement.
- **Buyers who refuse read-only access** to analytics, Google Business Profile,
  ad accounts, or call logs. Without evidence access, findings degrade to
  opinion, and the product promise breaks.
- **Buyers shopping for "more leads tomorrow."** This finds and fixes leaks; it
  is not a lead-generation service, and selling it as one creates refunds.

## 4. Audit Framework — Eight Categories

Each category defines **what is reviewed** (the checklist), **what is scored**
(the pass/fail conditions that feed the Revenue Leak Score), and **what is
reported** (what the customer sees). Every scored item is binary or three-state
(0 / 1 / 2) so two different auditors produce the same score — that is what
makes the product repeatable.

### 4.1 Website Conversion

- **Reviewed:** path from every major service page to a contact action; mobile
  rendering and tap targets; page speed on the money pages; visible phone
  number and click-to-call; quote/estimate CTA presence and placement; trust
  elements (license, insurance, reviews, photos of real work).
- **Scored:** contact action above the fold on mobile (0/1/2); click-to-call
  present site-wide (0/2); ≤3 taps from any service page to contact (0/1/2);
  money pages load <3s mobile (0/1/2); estimate CTA on every service page
  (0/1/2).
- **Reported:** annotated screenshots of the worst paths; the specific pages
  bleeding visitors; before/after mock of one fix (description, not design
  work).

### 4.2 Forms

- **Reviewed:** inventory of every form; live test submission through each;
  where submissions go and who is notified; confirmation behavior; field count
  and friction; spam handling; mobile usability.
- **Scored:** all forms deliver (0/2 — a silent dead form is an automatic
  category floor); notification reaches a monitored inbox/phone (0/1/2);
  response to our test lead within 15 min / 4 hours / never (2/1/0); ≤6 fields
  on primary form (0/1/2).
- **Reported:** test log with timestamps (submitted 10:14am → reply 2:40pm next
  day), each form's verdict, and the response-time gap vs. the 5-minute
  standard.

### 4.3 Calls

- **Reviewed:** mystery-shop calls during business hours and after hours; rings
  to answer; who answers and how; voicemail script and whether messages get
  returns; missed-call text-back presence; call tracking presence.
- **Scored:** business-hours call answered ≤4 rings (0/1/2); after-hours call
  has a capture path — answering service, text-back, or emergency line
  (0/1/2); voicemail returned same business day (0/1/2); any missed-call
  recovery automation (0/2); calls tracked/counted at all (0/2).
- **Reported:** the call log (date, time, outcome, recording where lawful),
  estimated missed-call volume extrapolated to monthly dollars using the
  customer's average job value.

### 4.4 Tracking

- **Reviewed:** GA4 presence and health; conversion events for calls and forms;
  call tracking numbers and pool configuration; GBP insights access; Search
  Console; tag hygiene (duplicates, dead tags).
- **Scored:** GA4 installed and collecting (0/2); form submissions tracked as
  conversions (0/2); calls tracked as conversions (0/2); GBP and Search
  Console claimed and accessible (0/1/2); one property, no duplicate/conflicting
  tags (0/1/2).
- **Reported:** "what you can and cannot answer today" table — e.g., *"You
  cannot currently count how many calls your website produces."* Each gap maps
  to a specific install task on the roadmap.

### 4.5 Attribution

- **Reviewed:** ad platform conversion wiring (Google Ads/LSA, Meta); whether
  lead source is captured at intake (forms, call tracking, "how did you hear
  about us"); whether any system connects lead → quote → booked job; spend by
  channel vs. measurable outcomes by channel.
- **Scored:** paid channels have conversion tracking wired (0/1/2); lead source
  captured on ≥80% of new leads (0/1/2); cost per *lead* computable by channel
  (0/1/2); cost per *booked job* computable by channel (0/2).
- **Reported:** the spend-vs-knowledge table: each channel, monthly spend, and
  what the business can actually prove it returns. This page routinely
  justifies the whole audit.

### 4.6 Lead Quality

- **Reviewed:** sample of recent leads by source (with the owner, during
  discovery and review); junk/spam ratio per source; geographic fit of leads vs.
  serviceable area; job-type fit vs. the work they want more of.
- **Scored:** lead source mix known (0/1/2); junk ratio per source known
  (0/1/2); leads predominantly inside the service area (0/1/2); intake
  qualifies job type/urgency (0/1/2).
- **Reported:** lead source quality comparison (volume, quality, fit) and where
  spend is buying leads the business can't or doesn't want to serve.

### 4.7 Follow-up

- **Reviewed:** what happens after first contact — speed-to-lead on our test
  submissions; second-touch attempts on no-answer; quote delivery time; quote
  follow-up cadence; any reactivation of aged leads; review request process
  after completed jobs.
- **Scored:** first response ≤5 min / ≤1 hr / ≤1 day / worse (2/1/1/0); ≥2
  attempts on unreached leads (0/1/2); quotes followed up within 48 hours
  (0/1/2); any systematic follow-up automation (0/2); review requests sent
  after jobs (0/1/2).
- **Reported:** the follow-up timeline reconstructed from evidence ("your
  average quote dies in silence after one touch"), with the recoverable revenue
  estimate — follow-up is usually the largest single leak.

### 4.8 Local SEO / Service Areas

- **Reviewed:** GBP completeness (categories, services, photos, posts, review
  velocity and response); local pack and organic position for [core service] +
  [city] across the *actual* service area; service-area page existence and
  quality; NAP consistency across major citations.
- **Scored:** GBP complete and actively maintained (0/1/2); ranks in local pack
  in home city for primary service (0/1/2); dedicated page per major service
  area (0/1/2); review response rate ≥80% (0/1/2); NAP consistent (0/1/2).
- **Reported:** coverage map — the cities they serve vs. the cities they're
  visible in — and the ranking snapshot per service-area/service combination.
  Directly feeds WSS service-area page work.

## 5. Revenue Leak Score

### Design

One headline number, 0–100, where **higher = healthier (less leakage)**. The
eight audit categories roll up into five customer-facing pillars so the
scorecard stays legible to an owner:

| Pillar | Built from (audit categories) | Weight | Why this weight |
|--------|------------------------------|--------|-----------------|
| **Conversion** | Website conversion + Forms + Calls (capture) | 30% | Leads that never become a contact are the most expensive leak — the marketing was already paid for. |
| **Follow-up** | Follow-up + Lead quality | 25% | Speed-to-lead and quote follow-up are where the most recoverable dollars hide, and fixes are cheap. |
| **Visibility** | Local SEO / service areas | 15% | Being invisible in half your service area caps everything downstream. |
| **Tracking** | Tracking | 15% | Measurement doesn't make money directly, but nothing can be managed or proven without it. |
| **Attribution** | Attribution | 15% | Connecting spend to booked jobs determines whether the next marketing dollar is informed. |

### Mechanics

- Every checklist item in §4 scores 0, 1, or 2 (defined per item above).
- Category score = points earned ÷ points possible × 100.
- Pillar score = the average of its categories (Conversion averages three;
  Follow-up averages two; the rest map 1:1).
- **Revenue Leak Score = Σ (pillar score × weight)**, rounded to an integer.
- Floor rule: a silent dead form or a completely untracked phone line caps the
  affected pillar at 40 regardless of other points — catastrophic single
  failures should not be averaged away.

### Bands (used throughout the report and the sales conversation)

| Score | Band | Plain-English meaning |
|-------|------|----------------------|
| 85–100 | **Tight ship** | Minor leaks; maintain and measure. |
| 65–84 | **Leaking** | Real money lost monthly; a focused sprint recovers most of it. |
| 40–64 | **Significant leakage** | Multiple broken stages; staged 90-day repair plan. |
| 0–39 | **Critical** | The lead path is structurally broken; foundation work before any new marketing spend. |

The same band drives the implementation recommendation (§8), which keeps the
report's conclusion honest and consistent across customers.

## 6. Report Structure

Single PDF, 15–20 pages, Corriston Consulting branded, written to be read by an
owner in 20 minutes. Structure:

1. **Cover** — business name, date, Revenue Leak Score badge, score band.
2. **Executive Summary (1 page)** — the score, the three biggest leaks with
   estimated monthly dollar impact, the three first fixes, and one sentence on
   what we'd do first and why.
3. **Scorecard (1 page)** — score gauge (0–100 with band coloring); horizontal
   bar chart of the five pillars vs. weights; eight category letter grades.
4. **Revenue Leak Map (1 page)** — funnel diagram of *their* lead path with
   leak points marked and sized (est. $/month at each stage). The
   most-screenshotted page; designed to be shown to a business partner or
   spouse.
5. **Category findings (8 × 1–1.5 pages)** — one section per audit category,
   identical layout: category grade chip → what we tested → what we found
   (evidence: screenshots, call log rows, timestamps) → what it costs you
   (estimate + assumption shown) → what to do about it (links to roadmap IDs).
6. **Spend vs. Proof table (1 page)** — each marketing channel: monthly spend,
   what's measurable today, cost per lead (if computable), cost per booked job
   (almost always "unknown" — the page that sells attribution work).
7. **Recommendation Roadmap (1–2 pages)** — every fix as a row: ID, fix,
   category, *Fix Now / 30 Days / 90 Days*, effort (S/M/L), owner
   (You / Corriston / WSS), estimated monthly recovery. Sorted by
   recovery-to-effort.
8. **Methodology & evidence appendix** — when we called, what we submitted,
   what we accessed, scoring rubric summary, and every dollar-estimate
   assumption stated explicitly (average job value, close rate, lead volume —
   sourced from the owner in discovery).

**Charts (kept to exactly four, same every time):** score gauge, pillar bar
chart, leak-map funnel, spend-vs-proof table. Consistency across customers
matters more than chart variety — it is what makes delivery repeatable and the
product recognizable.

Dollar estimates always show their arithmetic inline, e.g. *"~22 missed calls/mo
× 30% would-have-booked × $8,400 avg job × 45% close = ~$24,900/mo at risk"* —
conservative inputs, owner-supplied numbers, no black boxes. Credibility of the
estimates is the product's reputation.

## 8. Implementation Path — What the Audit Sells Next

The roadmap's *owner* column does the routing: every fix is tagged
You / Corriston / WSS, so the upsell is written into the deliverable itself
rather than pitched after it.

### 8.1 Fix-It Sprint — $1,500–$3,500 (Corriston Consulting)

The default next step for scores 40–84. Implements the *Fix Now* tier:

- $1,500 — measurement foundation: GA4 + conversion events, call tracking
  installed, form repair/routing, GBP completion.
- $2,500 — foundation + speed-to-lead: missed-call text-back, lead
  notification routing, quote follow-up sequence, intake source capture.
- $3,500 — full sprint: all of the above + attribution wiring into ad
  platforms + service-area page plan + 30 days of verification that every
  installed system is reporting truthfully.

**$750 credit:** the audit fee applies in full to any implementation engagement
of $2,500+ booked within 30 days of delivery. This makes "audit then implement"
cheaper than guessing, and gives the Day-40 check-in a built-in deadline.

### 8.2 Advisory Retainer — $750–$1,500/month (Corriston Consulting)

For owners who want accountability, not hands-on-keyboard: monthly review of
the now-working numbers, marketing spend decisions, vendor oversight, quarterly
re-score against the original audit. The audit report is the retainer's
operating document — every monthly call walks the same scorecard.

### 8.3 WSS Operational Work (Website Support Studio)

- Website-tagged roadmap items (conversion fixes, service-area pages, forms,
  click-to-call) route to **WSS Operations — $199/mo founder / $399/mo
  standard** as the ongoing execution engine.
- Audits that reveal a genuinely condemned site route to the **$500 Founder
  Website Package** (this catches any of the 72 that drift toward the 16).
- One-off items can consume WSS Capacity Unit top-ups where a subscription
  isn't warranted yet.

### 8.4 Routing by score band

| Band | Default recommendation in report |
|------|----------------------------------|
| 85–100 | No implementation push. Advisory retainer (light) or nothing — saying "you're fine" loudly is what makes the audit trustworthy and referable. |
| 65–84 | $1,500–$2,500 Fix-It Sprint → optional retainer. |
| 40–64 | $2,500–$3,500 Fix-It Sprint → retainer strongly recommended → WSS operations for website items. |
| 0–39 | Foundation conversation: possibly Founder Website Package + measurement sprint before any marketing spend. |

**Pipeline math to hold the offer accountable** (targets, not promises): 72
prospects → 15–20 audits sold in the first wave (~$11–15K direct) → ≥40%
implementation attach (~6–8 sprints, $12–24K) → 2–4 retainers and 3–5 WSS
subscriptions as the durable tail. If attach falls under 25%, the roadmap or
the credit mechanics are wrong — revisit before scaling outreach.

## 9. Final Recommendation

**Ship it at $750.** The price sits comfortably under an owner's
"needs-a-meeting" threshold, the 30-day credit converts it into a deposit on
implementation, and the audit manufactures its own pipeline: every deliverable
ends in a routed, owned, dollar-estimated next step.

Positioning rule: sell **"find out where you're losing jobs"** — never
"website audit." The Website Replacement Score already proved most of these 72
don't need a website; leading with the website re-opens the wrong conversation
and invites comparison with $99 SEO-report mills.

Productization rules that keep it repeatable:
1. **Same scope every time.** No custom additions at $750; anything extra is
   implementation.
2. **Checklist-scored, evidence-backed.** Two auditors, same business, same
   score — the rubric in §4 is the product.
3. **Same four charts, same report skeleton.** Template the PDF before selling
   audit #2.
4. **Show every dollar estimate's arithmetic.** Conservative, owner-sourced
   inputs. The estimates are the reputation.
5. **Say "you're fine" when it's true.** The 85+ band with no upsell is the
   cheapest trust-marketing the firm will ever buy.

Suggested build order (all design/ops, none of it touches production, Stripe,
or onboarding): (1) intake form + access checklist, (2) scoring workbook
implementing §4–§5, (3) report PDF template implementing §6, (4) pilot on 2–3
friendly prospects from the 72 at full price, (5) refine rubric weights from
the pilots, then open outreach to the rest of the list.
